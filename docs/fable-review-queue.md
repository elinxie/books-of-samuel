name for continuity with existing references, but it now means: items

M8 scope note (2026-08-27, Sonnet world-director scope pass — detail for
#27–#29 below). Opened alongside the M8 entry in `src/data/milestones.ts` and
`docs/design/nathans-oracle-brief.md`. 2 Samuel 7 is a fundamentally
different kind of chapter than anything M1–M7 covered: almost no narrated
physical action, and its central event (Nathan's oracle) is a divine
communication with no physical correlate at all. None of these block the
build; they follow the M6 #21/#24 pattern, where genuinely contested scope
and depiction-policy calls are decided at the scope pass and confirmed or
reversed at the milestone's own sign-off review.

**#27 — one small scene for 2 Samuel 7, not a cards-only/no-3D-scene
treatment.** The most consequential scope call this pass made, and a real
judgment call against a real alternative: 2 Samuel 7 has no crowd, no
procession, no death, and no journey between distinct sites, and a card
sequence alone could carry every fact in it honestly, with nothing lost —
that is genuinely true, not a strawman weighed only to be dismissed. The
`nathans-oracle` brief decided in favor of building a small scene anyway, on
two grounds: (1) a spatial callback only a 3D scene can deliver — standing
in the same enclosure `jerusalem-stronghold`/`ark-into-jerusalem` already
built, watching David's house finally shown complete after two milestones
under construction, and watching David sit at the same tent he danced in
front of; (2) near-zero marginal build cost, since almost every asset is a
straight reuse and the only new geometry is finishing an existing structure
plus two figures in seated/conversational poses. It is deliberately
reversible — nothing built here is expensive to unbuild toward a cards-only
treatment — the same posture #21 took toward the "no fourth Hebron scene"
call. **Confirm or reverse at the M8 build/sign-off review.**

**#28 — ADR-013 applied to a divine-communication event with no physical
correlate at all (Nathan's oracle, 2 Sam 7:4–17).** Genuinely new ground
within an already-decided policy: ADR-013 (ratified from queue #24) governs
narrated divine/supernatural events, and its two prior applications both had
something physical to withhold an effect from — 5:24's sound in the balsam
trees is a natural phenomenon; 6:7's strike on Uzzah has a resulting,
depictable death. 7:4–17 has neither: "the word of the LORD came to Nathan"
leaves no observable trace of any kind, no natural-phenomenon correlate, and
(within this chapter) no physical outcome either — the promise's fulfillment
is explicitly out of scope. The brief's resolution: ADR-013's stillness-only
default extends cleanly and applies more purely here than in either prior
case, precisely because there is nothing at all to hold back from
rendering — Nathan is shown settled for ordinary night rest, no receptive
posture, no light, no camera language implying presence, and the oracle's
full content is carried by card in the text's own first-person voice. Flagged
for confirmation because it is the purest test of the ADR to date and worth
checking that the policy's language ("carried by caption and by stillness or
the ordinary behavior of the scene around it") was written with a case like
5:24 in mind (something present to stay silent about) rather than a case
like this one (nothing present at all) — a future reviewer should confirm
the ADR's text doesn't need a narrow addendum for the zero-correlate case,
or rule that it already covers it exactly as read. **Confirm at the M8
build/sign-off review; if the ADR needs a one-line addendum naming this case
explicitly, add it there rather than reopening the whole ADR.**

**#29 — surfacing redaction-/source-critical readings of the oracle as
`scholarlyViews` — a new category of scholarly dispute for this project's
claim model.** 2 Samuel 7:13a ("he shall build a house for my name") is
widely read by critical scholars as a later theological insertion
anticipating Solomon and the temple, in tension with the surrounding
unconditional-sounding promise — a genuine, well-established crux in Samuel
scholarship. Every prior `scholarlyViews` dispute in this project has been
about identification (where was Kiriath-jearim), historicity/scale (how big
was David's kingdom), translation/manuscript variance (the 4:6 MT/LXX
divergence), or narrated causation (why did Joab kill Abner) — never about
whether parts of the received text as we have it were composed at different
times by different hands. This is a structurally different kind of question:
it has no visual correlate at all (nothing about a scene would render
differently depending on the answer), which is exactly why it's being
decided by disclosure-in-notes rather than staging. **Decided for this pass:**
the scene renders the received text as one narrated event (matching the
project's standing practice of narrating the text as it stands, not a
reconstructed compositional history); the redaction-critical reading is
carried as a `scholarlyViews` entry on `claim-nathan-oracle` if a named
citation is found (`mccarter-1984-ii-samuel`, once extended to 2 Samuel 7, is
the first place to check), and stays a disclosed but unattributed note if
not. **Confirm at the M8 build/sign-off review** — mainly to check whether
this project's claim-model documentation (`docs/reconstruction-method.md`)
should name compositional-layering as a fourth dispute type alongside
identification/historicity/translation/causation, or whether folding it into
the existing `scholarlyViews` mechanism without any documentation change is
sufficient. Low urgency; does not block the build either way.

**#23 — the _tsinnôr_ identification and the no-capture-route rendering bar.**
Decided for now, recorded here because the decision is contested elsewhere in the
field and because a future researcher finding could tempt a reversal:
`jerusalem-stronghold` renders **no capture mechanism of any kind** — no water
shaft, tunnel, conduit, infiltration route, scaling, or siege equipment, in any
mode — and stages only the approach and the aftermath, leaving the assault in the
gap the narrative itself leaves at 5:7 ("nevertheless David took the stronghold").
This extends the restraint the 2026-08-10 M5 sign-off ratified for killings the
text narrates without method detail (`hebron-reckoning`'s `AssassinPose` having no
strike field) from killing-method to assault-method. The _tsinnôr_ crux itself is
carried as `scholarlyViews` on its own claim. **Escalate before any change**: even
if #22 lands a strong citation for one reading, rendering a mechanism would make
the project's most-viewed new scene assert a resolution to one of the book's open
cruxes.

**#24 — depiction policy for narrated divine signs.** Genuinely new ground: the
project has never had to decide how to render a narrated divine sign, and 2 Samuel
5:24's sound of marching in the tops of the balsam trees is the first case that
sits inside a scene rather than inside a card. Default set by the
`rephaim-valley` brief: **the sign is stated, never visualized** — no wind effect,
light, canopy figures, timed or intensified foliage motion, or camera language
implying a presence; the beat is a held wait among ordinary trees and the caption
carries the text's claim as the text's claim. The reasoning is the same one behind
the anachronism rule: rendering it would assert as observed fact something for
which no observational basis exists, and would move the project toward the
sanitized-illustration genre it defines itself against. Flagged for a
dedicated ratification pass because this will recur constantly from 2 Samuel 6
onward (the ark narratives, 2 Samuel 7, 2 Samuel 24) and may deserve its own
ADR rather than a per-scene claim. Interim disclosure lives on
`claim-divine-sign-depiction`.
